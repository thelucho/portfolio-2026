# Thelucho — Creative Developer

Personal site of Luciano Dichiara, a creative developer based in Argentina. A living showcase of selected work, process, and taste — built to feel like a crafted experience rather than a static list of projects.

The site lives at [thelucho.dev](https://thelucho.dev).

## What it is

This portfolio presents more than fifteen years of frontend and creative development, much of it with Studio Hakuna. The focus is on tailor-made web experiences: thoughtful motion, lasting craft, and interfaces that feel alive.

It is the public face of the practice — a place to browse work, understand who is behind it, and start a conversation.

## What you will find

**Home.** An intro, a hero, a short manifesto, and a selection of featured projects, choreographed as you scroll.

**Works.** An archive of selected projects. Each one has its own case-study page with gallery, context, and objective.

**About.** Who I am, a compact career timeline, the tools I work with, and a few personal notes.

**Contact.** Availability for collaborations, agency work, and remote roles, plus a form to get in touch.

**Labs** is reserved for experiments and is marked as coming soon.

## How it was built

The site is a Next.js and React application, styled with Tailwind CSS. The visual language is editorial — closer to a designed object than to a typical developer template.

A few choices shape the feel of the whole site:

- **GSAP** is the backbone of motion: entrances, hovers, and page changes are timed and sequenced.
- **Lenis** gives the page a smooth, controlled scroll.
- **Resend** powers the contact form.

## What mattered in the making

Motion was never decoration. The goal was for animation to carry meaning — revealing, guiding, and reacting — not just filling space.

**The intro.** On first load, the name appears before the rest of the site is revealed, setting the tone without getting in the way.

**Page transitions.** Moving between sections is not a hard cut. A curtain covers the previous page and reveals the next, so navigation feels continuous.

**Scroll-driven storytelling.** Content unfolds as you move, timed so it does not fight the transition that brought you there.

**Interaction details.** A custom cursor, hover marquees, and small tactile moments make the site feel considered.

**Craft over novelty.** The stack is familiar on purpose. The work went into pacing, typography, color, and how motion sits with the content.
